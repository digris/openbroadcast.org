History

Open Broadcast is Switzerland’s first entirely user generated radio platform. It broadcasts programmes, produced by an Open Broadcast community on their own online Open Broadcast platform, throughout German-speaking Switzerland.

Not only is the content user generated; the production process itself is governed by the user who acts as an editor, checking and tweaking the format and accuracy of each piece before it goes on air. That way the user is responsible for the day’s output. It is this radical approach that sets Open Broadcast apart from previously established models of user generated radio.

Open Broadcast is non-commercial and operates according to the principles of open source, open content and open access. In the future Open Broadcast plans to invite other media providers to join the platform free of charge. By making the production and debate of media content public via its Internet platform, Open Broadcast supports the opinion-forming processes essential to any democracy.

How did Open Broadcast come about?

The idea for Open Broadcast arose during the one month radio project swissAIR. In February 2006 swissAIR accompanied the closing party of Zurich’s legendary Dachkantine with a series of live dj sets. This was such a success that Dachkantine co-founder Thomas Gilgen came up with an idea for a radio programme produced from home by an online community. Gilgen expanded and refined this concept in collaboration with (among others) subversive Basel businessman, programmer and author Georg Hasler.

In June 2006 – just after the swissAIR project – the Federal Department of Environment, Transport, Energy and Communications (UVEK) made eight DAB+ radio channels available.

On July 19th, Thomas Gilgen was granted a concession to run a radio programme for German-speaking Switzerland with “educational content, cultural topics and underground music”. He was asked to expand upon “traditional radio formats through experimental, multimedia” productions that explored the advanced data services offered by DAB+.

However, there was no software available for a community to create a radio programme from home via the web. The actualisation of this concept was dependent on the software – or the Open Broadcast platform. Our current Beta-Version (May 2010) focuses on the creation of playlists. It incorporates a user generated music archive, a playlist editor to compile full sets from single items as well as a scheduling tool for planning and production. Social network applications smooth the way for collaborative work. Tools for talk radio production will follow shortly.

What’s the idea behind the Open Broadcast community?

Open Broadcast is committed to user generated quality. Users are courted on an invitation-only basis. Personal histories are all important. Members who invite a new user act as his or her mentor to ensure that all user criteria and expectations have been met.

Our aim is to establish a competent community of musicians, music connoisseurs, artists, scientists and journalists; diverse expertise is brought together with media competency to create content. This community airs radio journalism that is free of ecological or political distortion, thus fulfilling the ideals of independent media. Every part of each production process is made public. Decentralised, un-synced production methods of online platforms allow users to take advantage of worldwide resources.

How is Open Broadcast different from other web-based, music-centred tools out there?

Open Broadcast is the only platform worldwide to legalise the uploading and downloading of music for the sake of radio production. The very principle of user generated production is based on open content opportunities that allow users to compile radio programmes offline from home.

Relevant metadata – release, artist, label – is automatically attached to all tracks or albums as they upload. This standardised data archiving system helps create assessment lists required by copyright collecting agencies.

We plan to offer all these tools to other media producers (radio stations) in the future and so aim to create an internationally relevant internet platform in the form of a media workshop.

Why open source?

Essentially Open Broadcast is a not-for-profit organisation. It was clear from day one that our platform software would be programmed openly and eventually made available to others. The open source principle invites developers to participate in the project and so enhance the software within an online community.

How does the platform work?

The platform’s community module is based on Elgg, an open source software for social networking. Elgg manages all classic social network options such as sending invitations, registering new users, creating groups for exchange and collaboration, sending messages, blogging, setting up activity lists.

Modules specific to radio production have been added to these basic Elgg options. They include a music archive that can be updated by community members, a playlist editor for compiling radio pieces as well as a scheduler that serves as a planning and editing tool.

The scheduler is linked to the Campcaster server, which transmits the playout. In the future it will be possible to access Open Broadcast music archives live in the studio via Campcaster.

Why did you use Campcaster studio?

Radio playout software on an open source basis is a niche product. It was essential to Open Broadcast to find a software solution that would be continuously updated by an active community of developers, as was the case with Campware. Campware and Open Broadcast enjoy a fruitful collaboration that has been deepened by the founding of Sourcefabric. Both Sourcefabric and Open Broadcast share the principle aims of independent media.

Where do we find Open Broadcast today?

Since January 1st, 2010 Open Broadcast has been airing a purely user generated music programme. Our user generated music archive comprises approximately 55,000 titles (April 1010) by over 6,000 artists and 2,000 record labels from which users have put together over 400 playlists. New compilations are submitted every day.
